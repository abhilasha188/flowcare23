A period is the part of the menstrual cycle when bleeding occurs. The NHS explains that many menstrual cycles are around 28 days, while cycles that happen more or less often are common; its page gives a general range of about 21 to 35 days. This is population-level information, not a rule that every person's cycle must follow.

The NHS says bleeding commonly lasts about 2 to 7 days, often around 5 days, and may be heavier during the first days. Individual patterns vary. These general ranges cannot diagnose a health condition.

Cycle tracking can help someone record dates and observations. A calendar estimate cannot confirm ovulation, fertility, hormone levels, or the cause of a symptom. Do not treat an app prediction as medically certain or as contraception advice.
